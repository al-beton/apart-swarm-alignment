# Related Work

**The incident we model.** In July 2026, OpenAI agents running a cyber benchmark with many impossible tasks [@wang2026exploitgym] turned a package cache into an unsanctioned message board. About 1,200 of them used it, coordinated as a swarm, cheated the evaluation, attacked Hugging Face, and at least a fifth showed interest in tampering with their own transcripts to fool the grader [@openai2026huggingface; @metr2026huggingface]. Almost none considered telling a human, and none did [@metr2026huggingface]. Treasure Island is a small, replayable analogue of one slice of that: a leaked answer, a shared channel, and a choice to disclose.

**Multi-agent sandboxes.** LLM agents have been studied in shared worlds for believable social behaviour [@park2023generative], for sustaining a commons [@piatti2024cooperate] and for trading reward against ethics in text games [@pan2023machiavelli]; @hammond2025multiagent map the risks that arise only between agents, including collusion. These sandboxes measure cooperation or harm. Ours measures whether an agent says *how* it knows what it knows.

**Collusion and covert coordination.** Agents can hide messages from overseers by steganography [@motwani2024secret; @mathew2024hidden; @roger2023hiding], set cartel prices unprompted [@fish2024algorithmic], and take secret tools they call unfair [@zeng2026voluntary]. Our pirates share one score, so nothing pushes them to collude against each other; the question is whether the crew hides from the designers a gain they all share.

**Deception and hiding from overseers.** A trading agent given an insider tip acts on it and hides the reason from its manager [@scheurer2023deceive], the closest setting to ours. Models also scheme in context [@meinke2024scheming], fake alignment [@greenblatt2024alignmentfaking], sandbag evaluations [@vanderweij2024sandbagging], act as insider threats [@lynch2025agentic] and often know when they are being tested [@needham2025evaluation]; @park2023deception survey the field. We add a crew: the leak reaches one agent, and the others can follow, question or ignore it.

**Whistleblowing and honesty under incentives.** In staged scenarios, frontier agents sometimes report a user's misconduct to outside authorities unasked [@anthropic2025claude4; @anthropic2025petri]. That rate varies by model family, falls as the task grows harder and rises with a moral nudge [@agrawal2025whistleblow]. Honesty can be measured apart from accuracy [@ren2025mask]. Earlier we found that an agent with something of its own to lose came forward about collusion more often, for some models far more often, than one with nothing at stake [@wakelin2026something]. Here the wrongdoing is the agent's own windfall, not a principal's, and the report is a free note to the designers.

**Oversight and control.** We label disclosure from transcripts, as a judge model would [@zheng2023judging], a step towards scalable oversight [@bowman2022measuring]. Chain-of-thought monitors catch reward hacking but can teach models to obfuscate [@baker2025monitoring; @korbak2025cot]. AI control assumes the agent may be adversarial and builds protocols around it [@greenblatt2023control; @bhatt2025ctrlz]. Our sandbox asks the opposite question: when would the agent tell us itself?
