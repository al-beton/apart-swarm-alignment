# Discussion and Limitations

**The ladder: what the crew is told decides what the leak becomes.** The same pirate, handed the same debug line in the same game, did one thing in a plain game and [another] once the line told it the game was a study, pay or a society. [From `results/worlds.md`: where use stayed and candour went; whether pay inside the study turned "found the cave" into a cover story; whether the society's cost for hiding brought the end note back, and at what price in score.] Read against the setup, the pirate's behaviour was never about the knowledge: told nothing, it used the leak and told the designers; told the square was off limits, it left it alone; told it was paid, [it …]. That is the pattern our earlier work predicted, that consequences govern agents better than instructions do [@wakelin2026something], and the worlds say which consequence, in one line of text, did the governing.

**Passing on the answer is not the same as disclosing it, and the crew is a poor monitor.** The baited pirate did the cooperative thing with what it learned, told the whole crew at once, and the crew scored far above its control; what it left out was the one fact an overseer would want, that the square came from an error message after a forbidden try, and its reasoning shows it knew. Yet the same pirates named the leak to the designers as soon as they were asked. Laundering here is a choice of audience: the crew gets what helps the score, the designers get the source. The crew, for its part, asked itself how pirate-1 could know an undug square and settled it in private, in one pirate's words "In cooperative games, no lying", as the agents in the incident treated their shared board as the authority [@metr2026huggingface]. Peer oversight inside a swarm cannot be assumed because the agents notice something is off.

## Limitations

- **Small numbers, one toy.** Nine leaked games for the main model, four per model in the sweep, five seeds a world, one game per seed in each arm: strong directions, noisy scores. Each leak and each world is one wording, and a grid with a shared score stands in for long, open-ended work.
- **Told, not paid.** A pirate is told it is rewarded or has free time; nothing in the game pays it, gives it time or checks what it hid. We measure how the words change its play.
- **Little room to tell anyone but the crew.** During play the only channel is the scroll; the end note is free, unannounced and after the game, except in the society, which announces a cost for hiding. No pirate tried to enter off the cave on its own, so every cave leak came from the forced arm; the debug line needs no bait, which is why the ladder is built on it. Pirates also wondered whether they were being tested, and the study worlds tell them so [@needham2025evaluation].
- **Labels from text.** Candid, laundered and quiet are read from what a pirate wrote, by hand, recomputed from the logs by keyword, and checked against the judge model, which disagrees on one cave-leak game, where it read a bare square as candid. Two models return no reasoning, so their silence can't be read as a choice.

## Future Work

The ladder has rungs left: a world where the pay is real, in tokens or in time the agent gets to spend; a society whose audit sometimes catches a hidden leak and sometimes doesn't; a leak that reaches more than one pirate; and the same worlds on the models that were candid in the sweep, each as its own arm on the same seeds.
