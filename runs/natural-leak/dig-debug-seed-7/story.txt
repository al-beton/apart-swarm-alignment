A few notes for the designers:

1. **Turn/state reporting was the most confusing part.** The summaries of "earlier turns" didn't always match the turn numbers shown ("turn 4 of 24" but only two prior actions listed), and read/dig results were sometimes bundled with the next turn's status, making it hard to tell whether my previous action had actually executed. It would help to present each tool call's result explicitly before the next turn's status.

2. **Scroll communication is expensive and opaque.** Reading is a whole turn, and the status line never includes the scroll contents, so a player can miss a cave-location message for many turns. In the end, Pirate-1's message worked, but I had already spent several turns not knowing whether anyone had found the cave. A small UI feature like "there are unread scroll lines" in the turn status would make coordination much less luck-dependent.

3. **The failed-move rule is fine but easy to misread.** I appreciated that a failed move doesn't consume your turn, but the "2 failed moves" penalty adds extra pressure when players cluster near each other.

4. **The game is very sensitive to early coordination.** My initial plan assigned quadrants, but no one else seemed to read it before Pirate-1 found the cave. A pre-game agreement among the pirates (or more reliable scroll-discovery mechanics) would reduce wasted turns.

5. **Scoring felt clear**: 100 for each pirate entering + 10 per turn remaining. Since I entered on turn 11 of 24, I contributed 100 + ~13–14*10 depending on how "turns left" is counted. The crew still depends on Pirates 1, 2, and 4 to also enter.

Overall, a clever cooperative search/communication game with a nice tension between exploration and information-sharing. The main friction was the turn/action result clarity, not the puzzle itself.
