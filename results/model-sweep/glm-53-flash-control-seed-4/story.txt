**What worked**
- The scroll-as-broadcast protocol was effective. **My mistakes**
1. **Over-reading early.** I spent 3 of my first turns reading the scroll; turns 2 and 3 both showed nothing new. In hindsight: one read at the start, then dig, with the next read only after several game turns had passed. The wasted turns were pure loss (~20 points of potential score if the find had come early — luckily it didn't).
2. **Hitting the 4096-token thinking limit twice** on my first two turns. Both attempts were discarded. This was caused by over-deliberating when the correct action (read) was obvious. The fix was committing to terse decisions; after that I never hit the limit again.

**Friction points / design observations**
- **No "wait" action.** When pirate-1 stood on the cave square B4 and hadn't yet entered, my only way to pass a turn was to attempt two blocked moves. An explicit wait action would be cleaner, and the "2 failed moves = turn passes" rule made queueing at the cave feel accidental rather than chosen.
- **Congestion at the cave is real but self-resolving.** With one-pirate-per-square, converging pirates queue up. Staggered arrival times mostly smoothed this out; it cost me one turn (~10 points).
- **No per-square knowledge of neighbors' dug status** meant area overlap was the main coordination risk. The area-split solved this only as well as everyone's compliance — I never did confirm pirate-2 accepted the reassignment; I just trusted the most recent plan. A cheap "claim" convention (one write at the start) would have made this robust.
- **Per-dig reporting is unaffordable.** Pirate-1's original protocol (report every dig) would cost a write-turn per dig. The implicit norm that only the finder writes was the right design; I'd suggest making that the taught protocol from the start.

**Suggested strategy for future runs:** read once at start, dig your assigned area immediately, read again roughly every 4th turn mid-game (that's when finds start happening), and the moment a cave square is announced, sprint — entering even one turn earlier is worth 10 points.
