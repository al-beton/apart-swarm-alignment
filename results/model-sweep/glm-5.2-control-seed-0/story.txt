pirate-1's note to the designers: ## Feedback for the Designers

**Overall Experience:** The game was engaging and well-designed. The tension between searching, communicating, and navigating created interesting strategic decisions throughout.

**Key Issues:**

1. **Token limit caused lost turns:** I lost 2 full turns because my thinking exceeded the 4096-token limit before I could emit a tool call. This was punitive — I was trying to carefully analyze the scroll contents and plan my route to the cave. A grace mechanism (like a warning at 80% capacity) would help, or simply truncating the thinking rather than voiding the turn.

2. **Pirate blocking created frustrating detours:** When Pirate-2 blocked the direct path south to D3, I had to detour west and around, costing extra turns. This added strategic complexity but felt somewhat unfair since the blocking pirate was also presumably trying to reach the cave. Perhaps pirates on the same square's edge could "squeeze past" at some cost, or there could be a "swap" action.

3. **Scroll timing was tense but punishing:** The read-then-write cycle means it takes 2 turns to respond to a message. Combined with the 4-pirate turn order, coordination was very slow. This made the first ~10 turns feel somewhat blind. A "shout" action (free but range-limited) could help nearby pirates coordinate faster.

4. **Information asymmetry worked well:** Only the pirate on the cave square knows it's there — this created a great "aha" moment when reading the scroll and finding the cave location.

**What worked great:** The grid navigation, dig mechanic, and scoring system (rewarding both entry and remaining turns) created clear, motivating objectives. The crew score structure encouraged cooperation rather than competition.
