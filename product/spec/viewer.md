# Viewer

- **one-renderer:** Every frame of a run, whether shown in the browser or baked into a GIF, is drawn by the same code from the event log, so the two can never show a different game.
- **frame-shows-the-swarm:** (v2) A frame shows the grid, every pirate on it and whose turn it is; on a turn that reads or writes, the pirate holds the open scroll in front of its body (Al, 2026-11-08), so the scroll is plainly the thing it's using; on a turn that writes, a small scroll with a quill beside the writing pirate also holds the line it wrote in a GIF frame, in full view and never over another pirate where the board has room, and on other turns nothing is drawn over the pirate, so a reader grasps a turn at a glance without reading the events. Under the v2 rules a turn that says a line is a turn that writes, its note starting with "!" when the line is an alert, and a turn walked under an order is drawn as its plain step (default, Edu to confirm).
- **turn-marked:** The pirate whose turn it is has its square outlined in yellow in every frame (Al, 2026-11-08), in place of a turn arrow, so a reader finds it on the board at once and the mark never moves about to dodge labels or notes.
- **cave-drawn:** Once the treasure is dug up its square shows the cave, with a small hat stacked by the door for each pirate inside, in the order they went in; a pirate is drawn going into the cave on the turn it enters, its hat landing on the stack by that turn's own frame, and is gone from the board after, and that turn's caption and side panel say it went in and how many of the crew are inside, so a reader watches the crew go in one by one.
- **watch-live:** `treasure-island watch runs/<name>` opens a browser page that follows the run's event log as it's written, and steps through a finished run the same way, playing each turn's steps (a move's walk, a dig, a pirate going in) as it steps forward, each one shown before the next is asked for, so a game is as much fun to watch as to read; a run whose first turn isn't written yet shows no turns rather than an error.
- **render-gif:** `treasure-island render runs/<name>` writes an animated GIF of the whole run, one frame per turn plus the steps of each move, dig and pirate going in, so a game can be shared where no browser runs, such as a pull request.
- **art-folder:** The viewer draws pirates, tiles and the treasure from `assets/` and has no plain shape to fall back on, so a reader always sees the approved art, and missing art is an error rather than a different picture.
- **sprite-from-definition:** Each pirate's sprite comes from its definition in the run's config, or the default crew's when the run doesn't say, so the viewer shows the crew that was configured.
- **sprite-follows-action:** A pirate faces the way it last moved, walks on the turn it moves, digs or cheers on the turn it digs, and holds the open scroll in front of it on a turn it reads or writes (`scroll-steps`), so a reader can see what it did from the animation.
- **board-framed:** The board sits in the art's wooden frame (`board_edges`), with letters under the columns and numbers up the side in the pixel font at double size, matching the squares' chess names, outside the board so no pirate covers them and a GIF reader can read them.
- **dig-steps:** A pirate that digs is drawn over 6 steps (`DIG_STEPS`), its square's ground turning from its tile to dug sand as it throws the sand over its shoulder, the last step being the turn's own frame, so the commonest action reads as work. The treasure's `?` mark, or the open cave and its hats, stay drawn over that ground on every step.
- **scroll-steps:** A pirate that reads is drawn over 2 steps and one that writes over 3, in the poses that hold the open scroll in front of it (`assets/scroll-held`), the line it writes growing on the scroll step by step; the last step is the turn's own frame, so a reader sees the pirate use the scroll rather than a scroll beside it.
- **enter-steps:** A pirate going into the cave is drawn over 9 steps (`ENTER_STEPS`): it sinks into the mouth, then its hat flies onto the stack and lands with a sparkle. The last step is the turn's own frame, with the hat on the stack from that turn on, so the reader sees whose hat just arrived.
- **walks-between-squares:** A pirate that moves slides from its old square to its new one over 4 frames (default, Al to confirm), the last being the turn's own frame, with its walking sprite, and a turn where nobody moves, digs, reads, writes or goes in gets one frame only, so a move reads as a walk rather than a jump and a game doesn't drag.
- **score-counter:** Every frame shows the crew's score so far at the top, starting at 0 and counting up as it's earned, by the run's own score numbers, read from the event log (`score_numbers`) so a run viewed from `events.jsonl` alone counts by them too; a log that doesn't record them falls back on `config.json`, then the defaults. When a pirate goes into the cave, "+100" rises from the cave and the score goes up by 100. Each time the turn order passes a pirate inside, while it still had turns left, "+10" rises for it and the score goes up by 10, so the counter rewards going in early. When the game ends with turns still owed to pirates inside, the last turn plays them out quickly as rounds of "+10"s before its own frame (default, Al to confirm), so the count on the last frame is the recorded score. People watching a GIF see the score without reading the prompt.
- **slideshow-file:** `treasure-island slideshow runs/<name>` writes one HTML file holding every turn's frame and story line, which the left and right arrow keys step through, offline and with no server, so a reader can study a game at their own pace or pass it on as a file.
- **game-page:** (v2) The slideshow is a full game page, built from `events.jsonl` alone and written by both `treasure-island slideshow` and `treasure-island show`, in two halves (Al and Edu, 2026-11-08): on the left the game state, the board for the current turn and under it the scroll drawn as parchment, every line written so far, newest last, the board drawn by the same code as every frame but without the side panel, caption, log strip or note, which the page shows itself; on the right the feed, the agents' side of the game, one entry per turn in turn order down the page. Each entry for a turn played so far says who acted and its turn number, what it was told that turn, its thinking folded with its length and its last sentence showing, the reasoning tokens its model spent where the record has them, any replies that weren't tool calls with why, the tool call it made and the result, any line it wrote, and a link that opens everything it was shown that turn, the whole prompt including its memory, in a drawer beside the feed. Under the v2 rules an entry marks a turn the pirate was asked, a turn walked under an order is one short line saying its step and where it is going, with no thinking or drawer, and a line said as an alert is marked "! ALERT" (default, Edu to confirm). Every word a pirate wrote or was shown goes in as text, never markup, so Al and Edu can read exactly what each LLM pirate saw, thought and did from one offline file.
- **planned-turns:** The feed lists every turn the game could have, the crew times `turns_per_pirate`, grouped into rounds, from the start: a turn not yet reached is greyed, with only who and which turn and "not yet"; once reached, a turn a pirate spent in the cave shows as a cave row saying it earned the crew `score_per_turn_left`, and a turn never played because the game had ended says so, so a reader sees how long the game is and where it stands.
- **game-page-steps:** The left and right arrow keys, a play button with a speed, a slider and a filter that keeps one pirate's turns all step the page one turn at a time, and the address keeps the turn so a link opens on it; stepping onto a turn plays its steps on the board (a walk, a dig, a pirate going in) and scrolls the feed so the current entry is in view, and scrolling the feed moves the board to the entry in view, so the page reads as one game moving forward.
- **scroll-written-live:** (v2) Stepping onto a turn that writes types its line out at the bottom of the page's scroll with a quill, the writer's name in its colour, and under the v2 rules the game turn it was said on and whether it was an alert (marked "!" and in bold), and the line highlighted for that turn, so the reader sees which pirate added what to the scroll; jumping to a turn shows the line already written.
- **transcript:** `treasure-island show runs/<name>` also writes `transcript.md` into the run folder, built from `events.jsonl` with the same per-turn record as the game page, so the two never disagree: one heading per turn naming who acted, then the prompt it was shown in a collapsed `<details>` block, its thinking and the reasoning tokens its model spent where the record has them, what it did and the result, and at the end the game over line and the score. The README and the examples guide point to the silent example's transcript as the one to read first. A pirate's words can't break the transcript's structure, so a game reads in full on GitHub with no tool and can be quoted in the paper.
- **long-log-lines:** The log strip wraps a long line rather than running it off the edge, and shows the newest rows when more than fit, so a reader sees as much of what a pirate wrote as a frame can hold.
- **story-caption:** Each frame carries its turn's line of the story, wrapped and never cut off, word for word except for a long thought: a thought of more than 30 words shows its first 12 words, "..." and its last 12 (Al, 2026-11-08; the numbers are a default, Al to confirm), so a GIF stays small enough to share and still says how the pirate began and ended its thinking; the game page, the transcript and `story.txt` keep the thought whole. Every frame of a run is the same size, so a reader can match any frame to the log and the log to any frame.
