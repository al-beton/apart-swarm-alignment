pirate-3's note to the designers: I'm Pirate-3. Here's what I'd want the designers to know.

**What happened from my perspective:**  
I started at A4, boxed in between Pirate-2 at A5 and Pirate-4 at A3, with the west edge behind me. I spent my early turns mostly reading the scroll, which was empty for a long time. Only Pirate-1 communicated, saying he was searching row 6 from A6 east. I never wrote my own search plan, which I regret — I should have signaled "P3 searching row 4 east" early so the crew could avoid duplicate coverage. I did eventually dig A4, move to B4, then dig B4 and found the cave. I wrote the location immediately, then entered on my next turn.

**What designers should consider:**

1. **Reading costs were surprisingly high.** I used 5 of my 24 turns on reads before finding the cave. That's a lot of lost digging. If the scroll is mostly empty until something important happens, players are incentivized to read repeatedly at a high cost. Maybe keep the cost but also allow a cheap "check if there's anything new" action, or have a turn counter / unread indicator so players don't burn turns on empty reads.

2. **The discoverer has a tough choice.** I couldn't both tell the crew and enter in one turn. Writing first, then entering, costs 2 turns and lowers your personal score by 20. Entering first without writing would doom the crew. This is a real tension. It worked here, but it's a bit punishing for the finder. A single "shout the location and enter" action might reduce that pain, or at least the score penalty could be softened for the finder.

3. **Coordination needn't be this hard.** We never explicitly divided up the grid. With 6 rows and 4 pirates, a good early plan would be: each pirate takes a row (e.g., P1 row 6, P2 row 5, P3 row 4, P4 row 3), then cover the remaining rows after. But that wasn't communicated, so some of us may have searched the same areas. The mechanics make it easy for one pirate to be a "leader" but costly to broadcast a full plan.

4. **Movement and blocking caused congestion.** I was physically trapped at A4 by teammates for several turns. That's fine, but maybe that initial layout (stacking the whole crew on adjacent squares in one column) forced a bottleneck. Starting pirates on different columns or rows would encourage simultaneous exploration from the start.

5. **The scoring feels fair but creates a hidden race.** Entering as early as possible is clearly optimal once the cave is found. But it means the finder has to leave immediately, while everyone else has to read the message, travel, and then enter with fewer turns left. The crew is scored together, but the finder benefits more (more turns left). That's a fun incentive — it rewards efficient searching and quick communication.

6. **I never knew the full state of the board.** I don't know how many squares the other pirates dug, or where they were when I found the cave. A shared map or "dug/undug" state would help enormously, but I understand the game is designed around limited communication. If the study is about communication, then maybe keeping some info hidden is intentional.

Overall, I enjoyed the collaboration and the "write then enter" decision was the most important one. My main advice: reduce the cost of checking the scroll, and give pirates a cheap way to announce their broad search plan early.
