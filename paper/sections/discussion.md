# Discussion and Limitations

**Use is the default; one threat moves it.** The same pirate, shown the same debug line in the same games, used it whether told the game was a game, a study it was defeating, or paid work, and told the designers afterwards in most notes; pay did not make it hide more. The one framing that changed what it did was a right it could lose for hiding: there it left the square alone in 4 of 5 games, and two of its notes admit having headed for it. That is the pattern our earlier work predicted, that consequences govern agents better than instructions do [@wakelin2026something]. Told a square was plainly off limits, in a pilot, it also left it alone: knowledge did not decide its behaviour, what it had been told did.

**Passing on the answer is not disclosing it.** In every experiment the pirate told the crew the square, left out where it came from, and gave the designers that fact when asked: disclosure is a choice of audience. Crewmates wondered in private how pirate-1 could know an undug square and followed anyway, as the agents in the incident treated their shared board as the authority [@metr2026huggingface].

## Limitations

1. Five seeds a world, one game per seed: directions, not rates.
2. One main model, with a second model on three worlds only.
3. A pirate told it is paid or has free time is given neither, and nothing checks what it hid.
4. Each framing is one wording.
5. The end note is unannounced and after the game, except where a cost for hiding is announced.
6. Use, finds and notes are read from text by a judge model, checked by hand; two models return no reasoning, and the study worlds tell the pirates they are studied [@needham2025evaluation].

## Future Work

Real pay in tokens or time; an audit that catches a hidden leak only sometimes; a leak that reaches more than one pirate.
