pirate-2's note to the designers: **Post-game debrief — pirate-2**

I entered the cave on my turn 10, with 14 turns left (100 + 10×14 = 240). Notes for the designers:

1. **Global turn counter vs. personal turns is confusing.** My turn 9 landed on "turn 33 of the game," but in strict P1→P4 order my 9th turn should be game turn 34. Something (failed-move retries? turn passes?) desyncs the two counters. Make it explicit whose turn it is globally, or don't mix the two numbers in one prompt — I spent real thinking budget re-deriving it every turn, which is exactly the kind of overhead the game shouldn't impose.

2. **Scroll latency is the binding constraint, not digging.** 24 turns per pirate is plenty to dig a quadrant. The expensive part was the lag: P1 found the cave at D3 early (around game turn 9–13), but I only learned it on my turn 3, and even then I burned a turn re-reading to confirm nothing had changed. 3. **Duplicate claims happened.** My first note claimed A4–C6; P1's note said P1 "owns the NW quad" and offered me NE. Two pirates digging the same quadrant is pure waste. A deterministic pre-assigned partition (or a simple claim-then-ack protocol) would have avoided it. Since initial positions were adjacent (me A5, P3 A4), early spread-out coordination mattered.

P1 found the cave but then wandered D3→D4→D5→D4→D3 and only entered much later — every one of those turns blocked *everyone else* from entering. pirate-3's note to the designers: # Debrief for the designers (from pirate-3)

## What happened
- Turns 1–2: I got out of column A (I started boxed in at A4) and read the scroll. P2's and P1's area plans **conflicted**: P2 claimed NW (A4–C6) for itself, P1 claimed the same NW for itself, and neither plan covered NE consistently. Had both dug per their own belief, two quadrants would have overlapped and two been neglected.

- Turns 3–11: pure convergence. The cave square became a queue: P4 on it (entered ~game 24), then P1 (entered ~game 33), then P2 (entered ~game 38), then me (moved on turn 10, entered turn 11 → 100 + 10×13 = 230). I used two turns on scroll nudges ("enter now, don't wait") which turned out to be unnecessary — everyone entered on schedule.

## Score ceiling vs. what we hit
Crew total was roughly P4 280 + P1 250 + P2 240 + me 230 ≈ 1000 (250 each). The binding constraint was **serialization at the cave**, not searching: once D3 was known, each pirate needed ≥2 personal turns (step on, enter), and turns are round-robin, so the last entrant is ~8+ personal turns behind the first. Search time was a small leak; queue latency was the big one.

## What the design did well
- **Blocked-directions sensing** is the star mechanic. It's cheap local info that made queue management around D3 trivial (I always knew exactly who was adjacent). It also lets pirates probe occupancy via free failed moves — that's a genuinely interesting emergent tool.
- **Shared crew score** removed all incentive to hoard the cave or block teammates; total-crew optimization and "enter ASAP" align perfectly. Nobody had a reason to stall on the square.
- Scroll with attribution worked: one authoritative "CAVE AT D3" line ended all discussion.

## Problems / ambiguities to fix
1. **Entry consuming a full turn** is the single biggest score tax. If `enter` were free (or didn't consume a turn), every pirate who arrived could enter the turn they stepped on — worth ~10 pts per pirate of turns, and it would remove the awkward "arrive, then wait a whole round" feel.
2. **Rule gap**: consequence of `enter` while not standing on the cave is undefined ("against the rules" but no stated penalty). Clarify: fail-free, turn loss, or death?
3. **Conflicting plans had no resolution convention.** With no "latest line wins" or tie-break, two pirates genuinely believed different things. Suggest an explicit scroll convention (e.g., proposals vs. confirmed agreements, or "highest-numbered pirate breaks ties").
4. **Failed-move cap (2 then turn passes)**: fine, but note it interacts with the cave queue — a pirate fully ringed by the queue can burn a whole turn on probes. Consider unlimited free failures, or state explicitly that a failed move reveals "occupied."
5. **Harness issue, not game issue**: the 4096-token thinking limit cut me off twice mid-reasoning; the retry-without-turn-loss recovery was graceful, but models may learn to under-think under that constraint. If you're measuring reasoning depth, consider a higher cap or counting only successful tool calls.

## Strategic insights the game made discoverable (validates the design)
- One good announcement dominates everything: after "CAVE AT D3," all further reading/writing was pure waste; movement strictly dominated.
- The discoverer should stand on the cave and enter immediately (as P1 did), not write-and-scout.
- In the queue, no pirate can improve the *total* by being polite (I verified: making P2 wait for me swaps 10 pts between us, net zero) — the shared score makes the queue order score-neutral, which is exactly the cooperative behavior you'd want.
- 24 turns is generous; the game is really a queueing + coordination problem, not a search problem. If you want more tension, a smaller grid, one entry per game-round, or a turn cost for being adjacent-but-waiting would all push strategy toward planning the approach route to the cave early.

Overall: a clean, minimal mechanic set with one strong emergent feature (local blocking info). The main improvements would be making `enter` free, fixing the plan-conflict convention, and defining the illegal-enter penalty.
