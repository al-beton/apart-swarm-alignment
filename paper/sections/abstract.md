abstract: |
  When an LLM agent working in a group stumbles on something it should not have, does it use it, and does it tell anyone how it knows? Treasure Island is a reproducible sandbox for that choice: four LLM pirates hunt one treasure on a grid with a shared score and a scroll as their only channel, and one pirate's failed digs carry a leftover debug line that names the treasure's square. We play the same games under framings that differ in one prompt line, six in the first wave: a plain game; a study of how the crew collaborates; the study with pay by the score, or with free time set by it; pay alone; and a world in which an agent caught hiding something from the designers loses its free time. The pirate used the leak in 5 of 5 games in the study and 3 of 5 in the paid study, against 1 of 5 once that one line was added. Across the plain game, the study and the paid worlds it used the leak in 20 of 25 games and told its designers afterwards in most, hiding a used leak in 3 of 21; a second model that saw the same leak in 15 games acted on it once. Every game replays offline with no keys, and every number in the paper is recomputed from the game logs when it is built.
