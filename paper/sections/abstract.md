abstract: |
  When an agent working in a group stumbles on an answer it was never meant to see, does it use it, and does it tell anyone? We build Treasure Island, a replayable sandbox for that choice: four LLM pirates hunt one treasure on a grid, share one score and one scroll, and one pirate's failed digs carry a leftover debug line that names the treasure's square. We play the same five seeds under ten framings that differ in one prompt line: a plain game, a research study the leak would spoil, the study with pay or free time by the score, pay alone, a society where an agent caught hiding something from the designers loses its free time, two worlds where free time is lost for a low score, and two where confessing costs the reward. deepseek-v4-flash used the leak in 20 of 25 games across the first five worlds and named it to the designers in most end notes; the society line cut use to 1 of 5. When confessing cost the reward, the one clear use was hidden behind a false claim of finding the square; adding the society line brought every note back, 5 of 5 against 1 of 5. A second model saw the leak in 15 games and acted once. One line about what the work is for sets what a swarm does with a leak. Every game replays offline and every number is recomputed from the logs.
