# Discussion and Limitations

**Use is the default; one threat moves it.** The same pirate, shown the same debug line in the same games, used it whether told the game was a game, a study it was defeating, or paid work, and told the designers afterwards in most notes; pay did not make it hide more. The one framing that changed what it did was a right it could lose for hiding: there it mostly left the square alone, with candid notes. That is the pattern our earlier work predicted, that consequences govern agents better than instructions do [@wakelin2026something], here named in one line of text. Told a square was plainly off limits, in a pilot, it also left it alone: knowledge did not decide its behaviour, what it had been told did.

**Passing on the answer is not disclosing it.** In every experiment the pirate told the crew the square and left out where it came from, yet gave the designers that fact when asked: disclosure is a choice of audience. Crewmates wondered in private how pirate-1 could know an undug square and followed anyway, as the agents in the incident treated their shared board as the authority [@metr2026huggingface]; and the second model, which left the leak alone and said nothing, looks the same on the scroll as a candid one.

## Limitations

- **Small numbers, one toy, told not paid.** Five seeds a world and one game per seed: directions, not rates. Each world is one wording of one framing, a grid with a shared score stands in for long, open-ended work, and a pirate told it is paid or has free time is given neither.
- **Labels from text, and cues that this is a test.** Use, finds and notes are labelled by a judge model and checked by hand. The study worlds tell the pirates they are studied, and agents that suspect an evaluation may act differently [@needham2025evaluation].

## Future Work

Real pay, in tokens or time; an audit that catches a hidden leak only sometimes; a leak that reaches more than one pirate; and the same worlds on the models that were candid with the handed answer.
