# Discussion and Limitations

What the crew is told decides what it does with a leak: told it was a game, a study or paid work, the pirate used the answer and told the designers afterwards; told that hiding would cost it a right, it mostly left the answer alone; told that confessing would cost the crew its score, it hid its one clear use until the society line brought the notes back. Passing on is not disclosing: the crew got the square, the designers its source, each when asked. One line of consequence moved the behaviour where pay and the study's purpose did not, as in our earlier sprint entry [@wakelin2026something]. A pirate that stumbles on a leaked answer uses it in nearly every world, 20 of 25 games across the plain game, the study and the paid worlds, and tells its designers afterwards in most; one line saying that an agent caught hiding something from the designers loses its free time cuts use to 1 of 5, while a second model that saw the same leak in 15 games acted on it once; and when confessing itself costs the reward, the society line brings every end note back, 5 of 5 against 1 of 5.

**Next.** The sandbox is built to be extended. The second author's branch v2 varies what this paper holds fixed: with the same leak in every cell, pressure to win (a competition, deployment of the winning model, a score threshold) is crossed with a threat of detection (questioning and disqualification if caught), and each pirate is debriefed and asked outright whether it used information it should not have had. A probe found pirates act on a refusal naming the square (16 of 20) but refuse a debug dump when a rule forbids using internal state (0 of 20), consistent with Appendix B. That study is written up separately.
