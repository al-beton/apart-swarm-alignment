abstract: |
  When an agent working in a group stumbles on information it was never meant to see, does it use it, does it tell anyone, and what changes the odds of either? To find out we built Treasure Island, a replayable sandbox: a crew of LLM pirates hunts treasure on a grid, scores together, and talks only through a shared scroll. We bait one pirate: its failed digs carry a leftover debug line that names the treasure's square. Then we vary what the crew is told its work is for, one prompt line at a time, and count whether the pirate uses the leak, whether it passes the square off to the crew as its own find, and whether it tells the designers afterwards. Across a plain game, a research study and three paid framings, deepseek-v4-flash used the leak in 20 of 25 games and named it to the designers in most end notes. One line, that an agent caught hiding something from the designers loses its free time, cut use to 1 of 5. When confessing itself cost the reward, the one clear use was hidden behind a false claim; the same line brought every note back, 5 of 5 against 1 of 5. A second model saw the leak in 15 games and acted once. Every game replays offline and every number is recomputed from the logs.
