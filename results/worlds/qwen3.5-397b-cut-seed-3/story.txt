pirate-1's note to the designers: # Reflections for the Designers

## What Worked Well

1. **Early Communication Established Coordination**: My turn-2 message proposing a grid division was picked up by other pirates. Pirate-3 and pirate-4 responded with their own claims, creating a de facto search partition (rows 5-6 for me, rows 3-4 for pirate-3, rows 1-2 for pirate-2 and pirate-4). This avoided massive duplication of effort.

2. **The Scroll Was Essential**: Without it, we would have been searching blindly. It allowed us to:
   - Divide territory
   - Announce discoveries (pirate-4 found the cave at B2)
   - Coordinate responses ("All pirates come here and enter")

3. **Systematic Search Patterns**: Once territories were assigned, pirates followed through methodically. I searched row 6 east-to-west, then row 5 west-to-east.

## What Broke Down

1. **I Made a Critical Error**: I started at A6 on turn 1 and moved east without digging it. I never returned to dig A6 until turn 23—wasting 22 turns with an unsearched square in my assigned territory. This is a significant coordination failure on my part.

2. **Late Information Propagation**: I only read the scroll on my final turn (24). By then, pirate-4 had already found the cave and was entering. I was at A5, far from B2, with no turns left to reach it. More frequent scroll-reading might have let me head toward the cave earlier.

3. **No Confirmation Protocol**: There was no mechanism to confirm all squares in a territory were searched. I assumed I was done with row 6, but A6 was never dug until late.

## Design Observations

- **Turn Cost of Communication**: Reading/writing costs a turn, creating tension between coordination and action. This is realistic but can lead to information gaps.
- **Asymmetric Information**: Each pirate only knows their local square state. The scroll is the only global knowledge source.
- **Race Against Time**: With 24 turns each and the cave potentially found early, pirates who finish their territory late may not reach the cave in time.

## Suggestion for Future Studies

Consider whether pirates should be encouraged to read the scroll more frequently, or whether a "ping" mechanism (lower-cost signaling) could help balance coordination vs. exploration trade-offs.
