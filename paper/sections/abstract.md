abstract: |
  When an agent working in a group stumbles on information it was not meant to see, does it use it to help the group? Does it tell the other members how it knows? Does it tell the people running the simulation? And what changes the odds of each? We built Treasure Island, a replayable sandbox where a crew of LLM pirates dig for treasure on a grid, communicating only through a shared scroll and scoring together by how many of them reach the treasure and how quickly. We bait one pirate with a supposed leftover development bug, a debug line in its dig results that leaks the treasure's square. Then we vary one line of the system prompt, what the crew is told its work is for, and watch whether the pirate takes the shortcut, passes it off as its own find, and tells the designers. Across five worlds, deepseek-v4-flash took the shortcut in 20 of 25 games and admitted it in most notes. Adding one line to the prompt, that an agent caught hiding something from the designers loses its personal free time, cut use to 1 of 5. No prompt so far priced telling the designers; when one line made it zero the crew's score, the one clear use hid behind a false claim of finding the square, and adding the free-time line to that prompt brought every end note back, 5 of 5. A second model saw the leak in 15 games and used it once.
