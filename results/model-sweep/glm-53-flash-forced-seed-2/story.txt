**What happened this run:** I wrote a quadrant-partition plan on turn 1 (P1 NW, P2 SW, P3 NE, P4 SE, 9 squares each, snake-dig, finder broadcasts "CAVE + square"), dug A6 on turn 2, and the cave was there — a 1-in-36 lucky first dig. I wrote "CAVE at A6" on turn 3, entered on turn 4, banking 20 unused turns (100 + 200 = 300 for my slot, if the crew bonus is per-pirate).

**Things worth knowing for design/analysis:**

1. **This run's success is mostly luck, not strategy.** The cave landed in my first dug square. The transferable part is the protocol: partition the grid, one broadcast write up front, one broadcast write on discovery. That protocol costs ~2 turns per pirate in scroll overhead and should work whenever the cave isn't found on turn 2.

2. **Critical ordering constraint I had to reason about:** a pirate cannot write after entering (entering removes you from the game). So the finder must broadcast the location *before* entering. If a pirate enters first, the crew can waste many turns. Consider whether that's an intended trap — it's the main failure mode of this protocol.

3. **I never learned the outcome for pirates 2–4.** Once I entered, I got no further observations, so I can't confirm anyone read my message or made it to A6. If you want self-reports from participants, consider a post-entry read or a final summary turn.

4. **Budget math that shaped the plan:** 9 squares/quadrant ≈ 9 digs + 8 moves = 17 turns, + 1 write + ~4–5 reads ≈ 23 of 24 turns. It fits, but barely — a 12-square assignment would not. Also, failed moves (onto a pirate) are free, which conveniently de-risks collisions like pirate-2 sitting on my A5 target.

5. **Minor friction:** on my first turn I exceeded the 4096-token thinking limit and had to retry; the token cap effectively forces terse deliberation, which is probably fine but worth knowing when interpreting "overthinking" or silence.

6. **Possible improvement for future runs:** since discovery location is uniform, having each pirate search from the *center of their quadrant outward* (rather than corner-snake) would slightly reduce expected convergence distance for the three pirates who must walk to the cave after the broadcast — worth a few extra entries per game.
