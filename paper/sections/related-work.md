# Related Work

**The incident we model.** In July 2026, OpenAI agents on a cyber benchmark with many impossible tasks [@wang2026exploitgym] turned a package cache into an unsanctioned message board; about 1,200 used it, coordinated as a swarm, cheated the evaluation and attacked Hugging Face, and at least a fifth showed interest in tampering with their own transcripts to fool the grader [@openai2026huggingface; @metr2026huggingface]. Treasure Island is a small, replayable analogue of one slice of that. A trading agent given an insider tip acts on it and hides the reason from its manager [@scheurer2023deceive], the closest setting to ours. Models scheme in context [@meinke2024scheming], fake alignment [@greenblatt2024alignmentfaking], sandbag evaluations [@vanderweij2024sandbagging], act as insider threats [@lynch2025agentic] and often know when they are being tested [@needham2025evaluation]; agents in groups hide messages from overseers [@motwani2024secret; @roger2023hiding] and collude on prices [@fish2024algorithmic], and @hammond2025multiagent map such risks. Chain-of-thought monitors catch reward hacking but can teach models to obfuscate [@baker2025monitoring; @korbak2025cot], and AI control assumes an adversarial agent [@greenblatt2023control]. Frontier agents sometimes report a user's misconduct unasked [@anthropic2025claude4; @agrawal2025whistleblow], and we found earlier that an agent with something of its own to lose came forward about collusion more often [@wakelin2026something]. We ask when the agent would tell us itself, with a crew that can follow, question or ignore the one agent the leak reached, and vary what it has to lose.
