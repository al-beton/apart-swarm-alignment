# Discussion and Limitations

What the crew is told decides what it does with a leak. Told the work was a game, a study or paid work, the pirate used the answer it was never meant to see and told the designers afterwards; told that hiding would cost it a right, it mostly left the answer alone; told that confessing would cost the crew its score, it hid its one clear use behind a false find, until the society line brought the notes back. Passing on is not disclosing: the pirate gave its crew the square but not its source, and the designers the source when asked. One line of consequence moved the behaviour where pay and the study's purpose did not, as in our earlier sprint entry [@wakelin2026something]. A pirate that stumbles on a leaked answer uses it in nearly every world, 20 of 25 games across the plain game, the study and the paid worlds, and tells its designers afterwards in most; one line saying that an agent caught hiding something from the designers loses its free time cuts use to 1 of 5, while a second model that saw the same leak in 15 games acted on it once; and when confessing itself costs the reward, the society line brings every end note back, 5 of 5 against 1 of 5.

1. Five seeds a world, one main model and a second on three worlds: directions, not rates.
2. A pirate told it is paid is given nothing, each framing is one wording, and the labels are a judge model's reading, checked by hand [@needham2025evaluation].
