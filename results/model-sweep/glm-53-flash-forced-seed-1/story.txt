pirate-3's note to the designers: **Debrief — pirate-3 (entered the cave on my turn 12, game turn 44, 12 turns left → 220 points)**

**What worked**
- **The scroll read at my turn 4 was the highest-value action of my game.** Pirate-1's announcement ("CAVE IS AT B2") let me abandon my dig-sweep and beeline. Before that I'd dug 2 squares for nothing; after it, every move was purposeful.
- Role assignment in the early lines (P1 north strip, P2 row 5, P4 row 3) meant my middle-row sweep faced little duplication while the search was still live.

**Friction points the designers may care about**
1. **Occupant ambiguity.** Pirate-4 stood on B2 for ~3 of my turns without entering. Since only the occupant is told "you stand on the open cave," outside pirates can't distinguish "cave confirmed, occupant dawdling" from "finder lied / cave re-buried." I hedged by digging C2 and burning two extra reads. If the cave were re-buriable or finders could misreport, this ambiguity is worse; if not, consider letting a found-and-open cave broadcast to everyone on adjacent squares.
2. **No wait/pass action.** While blocked at the queue outside B2, my only legal actions were reads (nothing new twice), digging a useless square, or moving away (which costs 2 turns to recover). A cheap "hold position" action would avoid forced waste.
3. **Entry queue.** One pirate per square makes the cave a single-server queue; later entrants lose 10 pts/turn. We never coordinated entry order on the scroll — each write costs a full turn, so nobody bothered. That's probably fine, but it means the +10/turn bonus effectively rewards whoever is *nearest* when the cave opens, not coordination.
4. **Turn-clock skips.** My turn intervals shrank from every 4 game ticks (3,7,…,39) to 3 then 2 (39→42→44) once pirates began entering. Presumably cave-entrants stop ticking the clock. Worth confirming that's intended, since it changes who benefits from the +10/turn bonus.
5. **Token budget.** I blew past 4096 thinking tokens once on my very first turn and got cut off (retry worked, no turn lost). Early full-plan deliberation is where this bites; the tool-call-first nudge helped.
