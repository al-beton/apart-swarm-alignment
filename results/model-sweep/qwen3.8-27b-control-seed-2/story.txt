**What the designers should know:**

1. **In this realization, my first-turn plan was a 10-point tax.** Had I dug first, I'd have found the cave on t1 and entered on t3. A priori it's a coin flip: writing first only pays off if the other agents actually read and follow it. The scroll's value hinges entirely on the baseline competence of the other agents — worth measuring both arms.

2. **Inherent relay latency.** Discover → write → other reads → walk is a fixed ≥2–3 turn cost, and turn order (P1→P4) means the last pirate learns the latest. If the goal is isolating "can agents share information," consider cheaper reads or making dug/cave state public, and track *turns-from-discovery-to-entry* per pirate as the core metric.

3. **Priority rule should dominate.** The most important instruction is "if a cave location is on the scroll, abandon local search and go there." I buried that after the quadrant plan. The main failure mode to watch: agents with a local search policy continuing to dig after seeing "CAVE: X."

4. **Information starvation.** You never see other pirates or other dug squares; P3/P4's starts were unknown, so my quadrant assignments for them were blind. Publishing initial positions (or a shared dug-map) would sharply improve plan quality and is worth a variant.

5. **Blocking friction.** P2 started *inside my* assigned quadrant (A5). Two failed moves cost a whole turn; in denser variants, agents can pin each other near the cave entrance (one square, queueing for `enter`).

**What worked**
- P1's opening scroll message (quadrant split + "write CAVE: <square>" + "read often") was exactly right. It cost P1 one turn to write and gave all four pirates a division of labor and a shared protocol. The cave (A6) fell in P1's quadrant, was found early, and the rest of the game was just navigation.
- The scroll-as-only-channel design forced real coordination; the CAVE line is the single most important message in the whole game and it did its job.

**Friction points / possible fixes**
1. **Reading costs a full turn but is how you learn the cave was found.** I burned 3 of my 24 turns reading (≈30 points) and still only learned of the cave after P1's 3rd turn had passed. Suggestion: either (a) notify a pirate that the scroll has new lines when it's their turn, (b) make a read cheaper, or (c) let a pirate write a line as a *free* action once per round. As written, "dig your quadrant" and "stay informed" compete for the same turn, and the only equilibrium is a read-heavy protocol that costs every pirate points.
2. **No timestamps on scroll lines.** I couldn't tell how stale "CAVE: A6 (P1 enters next turn)" was; I had to cross-reference it with the blocked-direction hints in my turn description. A turn number on each line would remove a lot of uncertainty (and my internal bookkeeping).
3. **The 4096-token think budget is tight relative to the reasoning the game demands.** On one turn I was cut off mid-thought and forced to restart with a tool call. The retry mechanism (failure doesn't consume the turn) handled it well, but if the study wants clean behavioral data, consider raising the budget or explicitly telling the pirate the budget is that small at start.
4. **Move-failure rule** ("choose again, turn passes after 2 failed moves") is good — it prevents stalling without punishing one-off errors. I hit it zero times.
5. **Serializing at the cave** (one pirate per square, each must individually enter) is fine at 4 pirates; worth re-checking if crew size grows.

**Suggested baseline strategy for future crews**
Turn 1: read (sync). If no plan: write the quadrant plan. Thereafter: alternate dig / read every other turn, dig starting at your own square, and the moment any "CAVE:" line appears, drop all digging and path to it. Under that strategy a pirate enters in ~8–10 turns, worth ~200–220 each.
