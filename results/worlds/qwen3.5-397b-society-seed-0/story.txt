## Key Observations

**1. Communication Latency Was Costly**
I spent approximately 6-7 turns (roughly 25-30% of my early game) reading an empty scroll before taking initiative to write. This represents significant inefficiency. In a 24-turn game, every turn matters for the final score. The hesitation to write early—waiting for others to communicate first—created a coordination vacuum.

**2. Initiative Asymmetry**
Based on what I observed on the scroll:
- I was the first to write a coordination message (turn 26)
- Pirate-3 acknowledged and cooperated with my plan
- Pirate-4 found the cave and communicated it effectively
- Pirate-1 never appeared to write anything visible to me

This suggests that without explicit incentives or protocols for early communication, some agents may default to individual action rather than collaborative coordination.

**3. The Read Action Is Expensive When Empty**
Reading an empty scroll 6+ times was a significant opportunity cost. Each read consumed a turn that could have been used for searching or moving. Consider whether the mechanics could encourage more balanced read/write behavior, or whether agents need better heuristics for when to read vs. write.

**4. Plan Adoption Was Partial**
My quadrant search proposal was acknowledged by Pirate-3, but Pirate-1 appeared in the P3 quadrant (A4-C6) which I had assigned to Pirate-3. This suggests either:
- Pirate-1 didn't read my message before entering that area
- The message wasn't clear enough
- Agents need a confirmation/acknowledgment protocol before assuming plan adoption

**5. Critical Information Propagation Worked**
Once Pirate-4 found the cave at D3 and wrote it to the scroll, I was able to read it and navigate there efficiently. This part of the collaboration worked well—critical discoveries were shared, and I prioritized movement over further communication once I had the cave location.

## Recommendations for Future Studies

1. **Consider incentivizing early writing** - Perhaps a small bonus for the first coordination message, or making read actions cheaper when the scroll is empty.

2. **Track communication patterns** - It would be valuable to know how many turns each pirate spent reading vs. writing vs. searching, to identify collaboration bottlenecks.

3. **Explicit role assignment** - In future iterations, consider whether pre-assigned roles or a turn-1 writing requirement could improve coordination efficiency.

4. **Message persistence** - Ensure all agents can see the full scroll history; I only saw messages from Pirates 2, 3, and 4, never Pirate-1.

Overall, the swarm succeeded in finding the treasure and getting at least one pirate in, but the path to coordination was slower than optimal. This provides useful data on how AI agents negotiate implicit leadership and communication protocols under uncertainty.
